## Individual Case Safety Report (ICSR)

*Illustrative case. Patient details are fictional.*

### 1. ADR identification

A 58-year-old man (82 kg) was started on levofloxacin 500 mg once daily for community-acquired pneumonia. He also takes prednisolone 10 mg daily for COPD and amlodipine 5 mg daily. On day 5 he developed pain and swelling in his right Achilles tendon. Levofloxacin was stopped on day 6, and ultrasound on day 7 showed a partial rupture. He was referred to orthopedics and fitted with a walking boot. The pain is easing, but he has not yet recovered.

### 2. Reporting

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Patient | Male, 58 years, 82 kg |
| Suspected medicine | Levofloxacin 500 mg oral, once daily, 5 days before onset |
| Concomitant medicines | Prednisolone 10 mg daily, amlodipine 5 mg daily |
| Adverse reaction | Achilles tendon pain and swelling, then partial rupture |
| Action taken | Drug withdrawn |
| Outcome | Recovering, not yet recovered |
| Report type | Spontaneous, initial |

The four minimum criteria are met: an identifiable patient, an identifiable reporter, a suspected drug, and an adverse event. Because the case is serious, it should be reported to the national pharmacovigilance centre within 15 calendar days.

### 3. Causality (WHO-UMC)

**Probable.** The reaction began 5 days after starting the drug, a plausible interval. Symptoms eased once the drug was stopped. Tendon rupture is a well-documented fluoroquinolone effect. Prednisolone raises tendon risk, so I treated it as a contributing factor rather than an alternative cause. A reviewer who sees it as an alternative cause would downgrade this to **Possible**. Rechallenge was not done and would be unsafe.

### 4. Classification

- **Seriousness:** Serious. Tendon rupture risks lasting disability and is medically important.
- **Expectedness:** Expected. It is a boxed warning in the levofloxacin label.
- **Severity:** Severe. It limits walking and needs medical treatment.
